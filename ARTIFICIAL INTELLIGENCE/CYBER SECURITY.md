# What is Cyber Security ?

## -> Cyber Security is the practice of protecting computers, networks, applications, and data from unauthorized access, attacks, damage, or disruption.
In simple terms:

Cyber security ensures that your digital information stays safe, private, correct, and available when you need it.

It applies to:  
Personal devices (mobiles, laptops)  
Organizational systems (servers, networks)  
Online services (banking, education, healthcare, government portals)

## Why is Cyber Security Important ?

Today, almost everything is digital:  
Banking and payments  
Education and exams  
Healthcare records  
Business operations  
Government services  
As digital usage increases, cyber threats also increase.

## Cyber security helps to:  
Protect personal and financial data  
Prevent fraud, identity theft, and data leaks  
Ensure business continuity  
Safeguard national and public infrastructure

## Core Objectives of Cyber Security (CIA Triad)
### Cyber security is built on three fundamental principles, known as the CIA Triad:
### 1.  Confidentiality
-> Ensures that information is accessible only to authorized people.  
Example: Password protected emails, private photos, Aadhaar details  
Prevents data leaks and unauthorized access

### 2. Integrity
-> Ensures that information is accurate and not altered without permission.  
Example: Exam results, bank balances, medical records

### 3. Availability
-> Ensures that systems and data are available when needed.  
Example: Online banking during business hours  
Prevents service outages and denial of service attacks

##  Cybersecurity Jargons
### Terminologies in Cyber Security
#### Cyber Security uses specific terminologies to describe weaknesses, threats, attackers, and risks involved in protecting digital systems and data. 
Understanding these terms helps learners and professionals clearly communicate security issues and analyze cyber incidents effectively.

## 1. Vulnerability
A vulnerability is a weakness or flaw in a system, application, network, or process that can be exploited by an attacker.  
Examples:  
Outdated software  
Weak or default passwords  
Unpatched operating systems  
Improper system configurations  
A vulnerability by itself does not cause damage, but it creates an opportunity for an attack.

A threat is a potential danger that can exploit a vulnerability and cause harm to a system, network, or data.  
Examples:  
Malware infection  
Phishing attempts  
Data breaches  
Unauthorized access

A threat represents the possibility of an attack occurring

## 3. Threat Actor
A threat actor is a person or group that intentionally attempts to exploit vulnerabilities to carry out a cyber-attack.  
Examples:  
Hackers  
Cyber criminals  
Insider attackers  
Organized cybercrime groups  

Threat actors may have different motivations such as financial gain, data theft, or disruption

## 4. Risk 
Risk refers to the likelihood and potential impact of a threat exploiting a vulnerability.   
In simple terms:  

Risk is the chance that a system will be affected by a cyber-attack.  

Higher risk exists when:  
Vulnerabilities are severe  
Threat actors are active  
Security controls are weak

Organizations use risk assessment to prioritize security measures.

## 5. Exploit
An exploit is a tool, technique, or method used by a threat actor to take advantage of a vulnerability.  
Examples:  
Malicious code  
Attack scripts  
Phishing links  
Software exploits

Exploits are the actual means through which cyber-attacks are carried out
